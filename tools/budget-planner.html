Building a budget that survives the first semester

Most student budgets fail in the first two months, when deposits, textbooks, a laptop and setting up a home all land at once. Plan those one-off costs separately and hold an emergency buffer of about three months of expenses; the planner shows that figure for you.

If you're studying abroad

  • Many countries require proof of funds for a student visa, often covering tuition plus a set amount of living costs. The official minimum changes, so check the immigration authority's site, and treat it as a floor, not a budget.
  • Work rules for international students vary by country and can change. Don't build a budget that only works if you can work more hours than your visa allows.
  • Currency moves can raise your costs mid-program. Add a margin of 10 percent or more if your income is in a different currency from your expenses.

Where students usually find savings

  • Shared housing, or living further out with a transit pass.
  • Second-hand or library textbooks and open textbooks such as OpenStax.
  • Student discounts on transport, software and phone plans.
  • Applying for small, local scholarships, which get fewer applicants.
