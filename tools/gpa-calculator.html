How GPA is calculated

Each letter grade converts to grade points. Multiply each course's points by its credits, add those up, and divide by total credits. That's your unweighted GPA. A weighted GPA adds a bonus for harder courses, commonly 0.5 for honours and 1.0 for AP, IB or dual-credit courses, so it can go above 4.0.

Schools and countries differ

Not every school uses this scale. Some award 4.3 for an A+, some weight differently, and many countries don't use GPA at all: Canada often uses percentages or a 4.3 or 9-point scale, the UK uses degree classifications, and India commonly uses percentages or a 10-point CGPA. When applying abroad, follow each university's own conversion instructions, or use a credential-evaluation service if they require one.

Tip for applications: report your GPA exactly as your transcript shows it, and let the admissions office convert it. Self-converted grades can cause problems.
