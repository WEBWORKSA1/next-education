What to compare, and what to ignore

The overall lean weights outcomes at 40%, cost at 30% and time at 20%, and subtracts heavily when accreditation isn't confirmed. Those weights reflect what usually matters most to a career decision, but your situation may differ, so read the individual winners too.

Questions that separate good programs from expensive ones

  • Is the school accredited or recognised by the body that matters for your country and profession?
  • How is "employed in field" defined, over what period, and what share of graduates responded?
  • What is the total cost including fees, materials, travel and income you'll give up?
  • Will credits transfer if you need to switch schools?
  • What support do online students actually get: advising, career services, tutoring?

Ignore rankings that don't explain their method, "limited-time" discounts that push you to sign quickly and salary figures without a source. More in our 12-point online degree checklist.
