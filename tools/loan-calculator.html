How student loan payments are calculated

Most fixed-rate education loans use a standard amortising formula: the same payment each month, with more of it going to interest at the start and more to principal later. The monthly payment is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the balance, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

If interest builds up during a grace period or while you're studying, it's often added to the balance (capitalised) before repayment begins, so you then pay interest on that interest. Toggle the grace setting to see the difference.

Country notes

  • United States: federal student loans offer income-driven repayment plans whose payments depend on income, not this formula. Check current plans at studentaid.gov.
  • Canada: federal and provincial student loans have their own repayment assistance programs. See canada.ca.
  • United Kingdom: Student Finance repayments are a percentage of income above a threshold, so this calculator doesn't apply directly. See GOV.UK.
  • India: bank education loans usually quote an EMI, which this calculator reproduces. Ask whether interest during the moratorium is simple or compounded.
  • Australia: HELP debts are repaid through the tax system based on income. See StudyAssist.

Before you borrow: exhaust grants and scholarships, compare at least three lenders, and run the ROI calculator with total interest included.

Loan calculator questions

Does this work for variable-rate loans?

It assumes a fixed rate. For a variable rate, run it at today's rate and again at a higher rate to see your risk.

Is it better to pay extra or invest?

Paying extra is a guaranteed return equal to your loan's interest rate. Higher-rate loans usually make extra payments the better choice; check for prepayment penalties first.

Is my data stored?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser.
