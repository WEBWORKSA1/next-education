Editorial standards

How we research and write, where our information comes from, how we keep advertising separate from editorial and how to tell us when we get something wrong.

Sources come first

We build guides from primary and authoritative sources: government departments and regulators, accreditation bodies, official study and immigration portals, universities and peer-reviewed research. Each guide lists its main sources at the end. We avoid citing marketing pages, unsourced statistics and aggregator sites.

We don't publish numbers that go stale

Fees, interest rates, visa thresholds, deadlines and salaries change, often every year. Rather than print a figure that will be wrong next cycle, we explain how the number works and send you to the official source for the current value. Where our tools use example figures, they're labelled as examples you should replace.

Review and updates

Every guide is reviewed before publication for accuracy, clarity and sourcing, and carries a "last updated" date. We revisit guides when rules change or readers report a problem, and at least once a year.

Corrections

If you spot an error, send a correction. We review every report, fix confirmed errors promptly and update the date on the page.

Advertising and editorial are separate

  • Advertisers, sponsors, partner schools and donors have no say over what our guides say or how our tools work.
  • Paid placements, such as featured program listings, sponsored guides and sponsored prizes, are always labelled.
  • We don't accept payment for positive coverage, and we don't promote pay-to-apply scholarships or predatory lending.
  • Ads served by Google AdSense are selected by Google, not by us, and don't imply our endorsement.

Use of AI

We may use software tools, including AI tools, to help with research, drafting and checking. Every published page is reviewed by a person against our sources and standards before it goes live, and we take responsibility for everything we publish.

Not professional advice

Our content is general information to help you make decisions. It isn't legal, financial, immigration or admissions advice for your situation. For decisions with legal or financial consequences, confirm with the official body or a qualified professional.
